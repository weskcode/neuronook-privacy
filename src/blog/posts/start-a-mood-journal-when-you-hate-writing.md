Many mood journals ask for a paragraph a day. If writing feels like a chore, that is the first thing you will drop. A mood journal does not need sentences. It needs the same small record, made on most days.

You can keep one in five ways with almost no writing.

## 1. One number a day

Pick a scale, such as 1 to 5, and rate your day. Write the number in a notes app or on a paper calendar. Keep the meaning fixed: 1 is low and 5 is high, every day. After a few weeks you can look back and see which days were high and which were low.

## 2. One word a day

If a number feels flat, use a single word. "Tired", "fine", "busy" and "good" all count. One word takes five seconds and still tells you something when you read the month back.

## 3. A mark on a calendar

Use a paper calendar and three colored pens, or three symbols. You decide what each one means. The page fills up over the month and shows its own pattern, without a single sentence.

## 4. A few yes or no questions

Choose three things to check each day. For example: did you sleep well, did you go outside, did you talk to someone? Answer yes or no. Yes and no questions are fast because you never have to find the words.

## 5. A photo or a voice note

Take one photo of something from your day, or record a ten-second voice memo. You can describe the day out loud without typing. Listen back later if you want to.

## Tips that help any method

Pick one method and use it for two weeks before you change it. Changing methods every few days makes the records hard to compare.

Tie the check-in to something you already do, such as brushing your teeth or plugging in your phone. If you miss a day, skip it and carry on. A gap in the record is fine.

Look back once a week. Read the numbers or words, and notice what repeats.
