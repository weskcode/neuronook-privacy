A daily check-in is a short set of questions you ask yourself at about the same time each day. The questions below cover six areas: sleep, stress, mood, motivation, social connection and energy. Pick a few, or use all 25.

These questions are for reflection. They are not a test, and no answer is a wrong one.

## How to use them

Answer each question with a number from 1 to 5, or with one word. If something stands out, write one line about it. If nothing does, skip the line.

Ask them at the same point each day, such as after dinner or while you brush your teeth. A fixed spot makes the habit easier to keep. If you miss a day, leave the gap and start again the next day.

## Sleep

1. How did you sleep last night?
2. How rested did you feel when you woke up?
3. Did you go to bed and wake up around the times you wanted?
4. What got in the way of your sleep, if anything did?

## Stress

5. How stressed did you feel today?
6. When was your stress highest?
7. How well did you manage it?
8. What is one thing that would make tomorrow a little lighter?

## Mood

9. How was your overall mood today?
10. Did your mood change during the day? When?
11. How emotionally balanced did you feel?
12. What is one word for today?

## Motivation

13. How motivated did you feel to work on your goals?
14. What was easiest to start today?
15. What was hardest to start?
16. What is one small thing you finished?

## Social connection

17. How connected did you feel with other people today?
18. Who did you talk to?
19. Did you want more time with people, or more time alone?
20. Who would you like to reach out to this week?

## Energy

21. How was your energy through the day?
22. When did you feel most awake?
23. When did your energy drop?
24. Did you take a break when you needed one?

## One to finish

25. What is one thing you want to remember about today?

## Answering all 25 in about a minute

You do not need to answer every question every day. Use the first question in each area as your daily set, which gives you six answers. Add the others on days when you want more detail.

Keep the same scale each time. If 1 means "low" and 5 means "high" today, it must mean the same next week. That way you can compare days later.

## Turning the questions into a game

NeuroNook asks nine of these kinds of questions each day, one at a time, across the same six areas. Three examples: "How was your sleep quality last night?", "How stressed did you feel today?" and "How connected did you feel with others today?"

Instead of typing, you answer by playing a short construction mini-game, such as tapping a brick to fill a gap in a wall. Each finished check-in adds a house to your seven-day neighborhood. With NeuroNook Plus you can add up to three questions of your own, such as "Did you go outside today?"
